Questions, answered

Free AI D&D — no fine print

Is this AI D&D roleplaying game really free?

Yes. The Lore Weaver's Cauldron is free to play — no subscription, no credit card, no email required. You get 50 fresh turns every single day.

How many free turns do I get each day?

50 fresh turns every day — more daily free turns than any other AI Dungeon Master. Each turn is one action or reply in your campaign.

What is an AI Dungeon Master?

An AI Dungeon Master is an LLM that runs your D&D campaign — narrating the world, keeping strict 5e rules, and remembering your story across sessions. The Cauldron pairs it with a persistent world engine, real dice ceremony, and AI-painted portraits.

Does the AI Dungeon Master remember my campaign?

Yes — the Lorekeeper engine tracks story threads, characters, growth, quests and NPCs across every session, so the innkeeper still remembers what you did last season.

What’s new in the realm

v1.3 — The Rewoven Thread · updated 2026-09-21
  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash — new on the Patron path 2026-09-21 — DeepSeek’s open-weights reasoning model (552B, 16B active, 1M-token context, MIT license) joins the Patron tier as a player-chosen Dungeon Master. It replaces DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731.
  • Meet Step 5 Preview — new in the free tier Try it 2026-09-20 — StepFun’s Step 5 Preview joins the free tier. Every account can set it as their Dungeon Master — no Patron coin required. Pick it at the model gate when you start an adventure, or switch to it in Settings.
  • Qwen 3.8 Flash — in the free tier 2026-09-13 — free for every account, no Patron coin required. Set it as your Dungeon Master in Settings, or start a fresh adventure and pick it from the model list.
  • Give your composer room New 2026-09-13 — the divider between the DM's log and your own words now drags to resize (double-click resets it), and the box has generous space on desktop and mobile alike — long roleplay replies no longer vanish behind a pinhole.
  • Combat that keeps its own count Improved 2026-09-13 — rounds now turn over by themselves once everyone has acted, enemies of the same name can no longer share one action flag, and each round closes with a single honest health snapshot instead of a stream of stale numbers. The voice picker also shows free players only the voices they can actually use.
  • Undo your last turn New — mistyped a line, or changed your mind the moment you hit send? The last message, the DM's reply and every change the Lorekeeper recorded from it are rewound together: inventory, gold, HP, quests, the journal and the memory return to where they stood. It costs one turn, and only the most recent turn can be rewound.
  • The party portrait blends again Fixed — the character card had gone opaque and left the portrait as a small square beside it; the image now sits behind the name, class and health bars the way it used to.
  • Your blade rolls, the DM does not New — weapon damage now tumbles through the server's dice engine on every hit, doubled on a critical; enemy blows have always worked that way. The story decides what it feels like, the dice decide what it costs.
  • Conditions that actually bind New — prone, restrained, stunned and poisoned now change rolls, turns and HP between rounds. A knocked-down goblin is easier to hit, and it hits worse.
  • Level-ups that level up New — reaching a new tier grants hit-dice health, class features, spell slots and a proficiency bonus that finally scales with who you have become.
  • A rulebook that matches the machine — the mechanics page was audited line by line against the code and rewritten where it promised more than it delivered; spell slots and rests are documented at last.
  • Truer day-to-day play — armour now moves your AC, long rests hand back half your hit dice, quests pay what they promise, and a purse can no longer be quietly rounded to zero.
  • One gold across the realm — the palette was consolidated into shared tokens, HP bars update on every combat turn, and offline rolls announce themselves honestly.
  • Returning heroes skip the tutorial — the onboarding steps fold away once you have an adventure, and the realm speaks your language through the whole quota ledger.

About the game creator

About the game creator

The Lore Weaver’s Cauldron is a solo project — and it was vibed, from first sketch to final spell. Every line of code was written in conversation between me and AI models. No team, no boilerplate — just me, the models, and an idea that refused to let go.

I’m a D&D dad. I started building this for a friend — a small experiment to see if an AI could actually be a dungeon master. It turned into something much bigger: a persistent world with its own lorekeeper, its own dice, its own stories.

Every API cost of this game is mine. Every word the DM writes, every portrait the engines paint, every reasoning token — I pay for it all. That’s what keeps the game free for you, and keeps it honest: no investors, no ads, no data deals.

And the whole thing runs on a small mini-PC in Stockholm — no cloud provider, no server farm. One little box on a desk is holding every story told at this table.

It’s free to play, because it started as a gift. If it gives you a story worth telling, that’s payment enough.

— rostad
