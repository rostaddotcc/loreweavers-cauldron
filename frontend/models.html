The Minds Behind the Mask

✦

Four minds can sit at your table — and behind them wait the voices and the brushes. Each DM is a different weight of thought, a different pace of speech; the narrators give the realm a voice, and the brushes give it faces. Here is what each one is made of, and what it is best at.
Powered by Qwen, DeepSeek, StepFun & Wan

⚙

How the Table Works

🧙The Dungeon Master — the voice you hear. Every turn, your words go to the DM model, which narrates the world, plays the NPCs and drives the story. This is the model you choose at the model gate when you start a campaign — and you can change it at any time in the settings.
🦉The Lorekeeper (Guardian) — the quiet bookkeeper. After every exchange, a separate model reads the scene and updates the world state: HP, gold, items, XP, quests, locations. It works in the background, so you never have to track numbers yourself — the world simply remembers.
✎The Extraction model — the scribe that turns free-form actions into mechanics. When you write "I draw my sword and lunge", extraction decides what that means in dice terms, damage and consequence — keeping the 5e rules honest even when you don't use the exact phrasing.
⏱Response time — heavier minds think longer. A turn with the fast StepFun 3.7 Flash usually answers in a few seconds; Step 5 Preview may pause several seconds more because it reasons deeply before it speaks. The Lorekeeper and Extraction run in parallel after the DM replies, so they rarely add visible wait — but a long, action-heavy turn can take a little longer overall. Pick the fastest mind that still feels like your storyteller.

Bring Your Own Breath — Local AI

🏮
Ollama — Models on Your Own Machine
Runs where you run it · open source
Local DM

The Cauldron can now speak with a voice that lives on your own hardware. The server stays the brain — it builds every prompt and keeps the books: dice, HP, combat and the world state are rolled and owned server-side, exactly as before. Your machine becomes the mouth: the browser asks your local Ollama to narrate and hands the text back. No API key exists for Ollama, so there is nothing to store, nothing to leak, and the DM step costs 0 turns — it isn't our electricity anymore. It isn't our content policy either: the house minds (StepFun, Qwen, DeepSeek) are subject to their providers' policies, while a model on your hardware answers only to you. Any model your Ollama can run is fully playable as the DM — uncensored ones included. Images and voice remain house engines; this is about the DM's words.

①Install Ollama on the machine at your table — curl -fsSL https://ollama.com/install.sh | sh, then ollama pull qwen3:8b (or a bigger model you have VRAM for).
②Open the picker. Settings (⚙️) at the table shows a 🏮 group with the models it finds on your machine, a ⟳ refresh, and a context slider. If your Ollama listens somewhere unusual — another port, another box on the LAN — paste the base URL (e.g. http://192.168.1.40:11434) in the settings field; it is saved in your browser only and never sent to us.
③Play. Pick a 🏮 model as your DM. Your browser streams the narration token by token from your own machine; everything else at the table behaves the same.
⚑Requirements: desktop Chrome or Firefox on the same machine as Ollama — loopback to your own box is what this is built for. From another machine (or your phone): Ollama on OLLAMA_HOST=0.0.0.0 with OLLAMA_ORIGINS=https://dnd.rostad.cc, and TLS on its address — browsers refuse plain-HTTP LAN calls from an HTTPS page, so on a tailnet put tailscale serve in front of it. If the page finds your Ollama but shows a yellow CORS dot, that origins line is the fix. The bookkeeping roles want a model that follows JSON-like instructions — a 14B+ thinking model does those steps happily, a tiny 3B one fumbles them.
④Pick who carries the background — role by role. The 🏮 group sits in three dropdowns: Dungeon Master, Lorekeeper (the Guardian: rules, dice checks, HP/XP/items bookkeeping) and Background (extraction: facts, journal, memory) — in the ⚙ settings and in the new-adventure setup alike, once you've opted in with Use own Ollama. There is no mode switch: you simply pick your own model for as many roles as you like, and roles you leave on house models keep running on the house exactly as before. When both the Lorekeeper and the Background run locally, the daily allowance rises to roughly 300 messages, because the house then spends almost nothing per turn; it keeps the periodic summaries and the logbook either way.
◆Your table, your rules. House minds answer to their providers' content policies; a model on your own hardware answers to you. Whatever your Ollama can run is fully playable as the DM — the uncensored ones included — and the house doesn't get a vote in what it says. It does still keep the books: the server builds every prompt, and dice, HP and the Lorekeeper stay server-authoritative, so a local DM can be as frank as it likes without the numbers drifting. Run the Lorekeeper and the Background locally too and the whole Cauldron breathes on your machine. Portraits and voices are the exception — image generation and TTS stay on house engines.

The honest limitations. The daily allowance follows how many roles you run at home: while any background role still rides our models, local play is capped at roughly 100 local messages per account per day — still about three times the free tier's play, for none of the DM-step cost. Pick 🏮 models for both the Lorekeeper and the Background and the cap lifts to roughly 300 messages a day — which is simply because our API cost per turn drops to about zero there. One dependency, honestly stated: local background roles only take effect when the DM is local too — the relay lives on that path. Periodic summaries and the logbook stay on the house either way, and voice narration and images are not local features — they keep costing house turns as before. Narration quality is exactly as good as the model you picked and as fast as your hardware: an 8B model tells a serviceable tale, a sub-8B one will lose the plot of a long campaign, and a 14B+ thinking model on a real GPU is where this stops feeling like a trade-off — the bookkeeping roles ask even more instruction-following, since their steps come back as JSON-like tasks. The prompt your Ollama receives is the same prompt our DMs get — it is deliberately not a secret; you can inspect what your DM was actually asked. And a local campaign is your own hardware's child: dice, HP and combat remain server-authoritative, so nothing fabricated locally changes the books. The whole feature is opt-in — nothing is probed until you tick Use own Ollama. 🏮 Bring Your Own Breath — the full player guide covers setup, the CORS trap behind the yellow dot, and the troubleshooting FAQ.
